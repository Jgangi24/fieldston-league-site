Mogul Mike: the most talked-about man in two arenas

Rubber ducks made him rich, courtside seats made him famous, and a running feud with the men in stripes has made him a weekly storyline all his own

By Larry Kuppleton

Fieldston Core Four Fantasy League · August 5, 2026

Every league has a best player. This one also has a main character, and he doesn't even suit up.

Mike Wiltworthy Kobronabitch — “Mogul Mike” to the box scores he's constantly interrupting — owns two franchises in this league, the Los Angeles Magic Wiltworthy Kobronabitches and the Laurel Canyon Naughty Chauffeurs, and treats both like his personal stage. Tinted aviators, a wardrobe that appears to only come in black or velvet, and a permanent spot in the first row directly behind whichever bench is losing its composure fastest. He didn't buy his way into two locker rooms. He bought his way onto the broadcast.

The fortune, for anyone who's somehow missed the origin story by now, is rubber ducks. Specifically, a line of them coated in a proprietary yellow dust that reviewers, competitors, and eventually a fairly large number of state attorneys general have all independently described as “strange to the point of concerning.” Kids wouldn't put them down. Some still won't. Mogul Mike maintains, across several ongoing lawsuits and at least one deposition that reportedly required a recess, that the dust is “completely inert and frankly just good marketing.” The checks cleared either way, and two courtside season packages followed shortly after.

Those seats are where the character really takes shape. He's been photographed mid-laugh with half the league's celebrity row, mid-argument with officials who did not ask for his opinion, and at least once mid-air, hoisted onto a player's shoulders during a celebration that started on the court and, by all accounts, was not supposed to include the owner. Ask the referees which version they'd prefer to see less of and the answer arrives without hesitation.

The court-storming is its own running bit at this point. A blown call, real or perceived, and Mogul Mike is out of his seat, arms out, fully convinced he's about to be the one who talks a zebra out of a whistle. It has never once worked. It has also never once stopped him from trying again the very next questionable call, which is either stubbornness or the most consistent part of his whole personality.

The chumminess with his own players draws the loudest criticism, and it isn't hard to see why. Owners are supposed to sit in a suite and sign checks, not get carried around a confetti-filled court like a conquering hero. But talk to the locker room and the story bends a little: the guy shows up, he means it when he celebrates, and whatever the lawsuits eventually decide about the yellow dust, nobody's questioning whether he's actually having the time of his life doing this.

Two teams, one unmistakable presence, and a legal team that's earning every cent of whatever he's paying them. That's Mogul Mike — impossible to miss, and this league wouldn't be the same without him.
