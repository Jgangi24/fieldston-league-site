The long Rose back to redemption

Four straight wins, a 36-point night, and a player the real game moved on from too early finally getting his runway

By Larry Kuppleton

Fieldston Core Four Fantasy League · August 4, 2026

There's a version of this story where it ends in 2012, on a torn ACL, with the league's youngest MVP never quite becoming what everyone thought he'd become. That version is real. It happened. It's not going anywhere.

This is the other version.

Over the last four games, Derrick Rose has been the best player on the floor for the Flamington Tuxedos, and the Tuxedos have not lost. Twenty-five points against Los Angeles. Thirty-six — on 9-of-11 shooting — against Queens. Twenty-two more against a Magic Wiltworthy Kobronabitches team that had no answer for him. Twenty in a physical, grinding win over Blankendorf. Four games, 103 points, and four times, the Tuxedos walked off with the win.

The 36-point night stands alone. Nine field goals on eleven attempts is the kind of efficiency that doesn't happen by accident, and it came paired with 16-of-18 from the free-throw line — Rose getting to the rim, drawing contact, and simply not missing. It's the best game anyone has played in this league all season, by anyone's box score.

“He's been unguardable,” said teammate James Harden, who's had his own strong stretch riding shotgun. “Every night it's a different way he beats you — pull-up, downhill, getting to the line. You pick your poison and he makes you pay for the other one.”

Kevin Garnett, who's provided real punch of his own during the streak, put it more bluntly. “Man's playing like he's got something to prove. I don't know what it is, but I hope he keeps proving it.”

Tuxedos owner Jess Gangi doesn't hide her enthusiasm either. “He was a value pick, honestly — I don't think anyone in that draft room thought they were getting this,” Gangi said. “But that's what determination looks like. It means something to this organization, and I think it means something to the fans watching, too — you're seeing a guy get the runway he never fully got the first time around.”

There's a real story underneath the numbers, and it isn't really about this league at all. Rose was the youngest MVP in NBA history, and when the actual NBA sat down to name its 75 greatest players ever, he wasn't one of them — the only MVP left off that list. The reason wasn't forgetting. It was arithmetic: a torn ACL in the first round of the 2012 playoffs took the explosion that made him unguardable, and it never fully came back.

It's worth sitting with the company he's kept in that specific injustice. Bill Walton's prime lasted barely three healthy seasons — a body that betrayed him again and again — and yet Walton won a regular-season MVP of his own and still made the 75th Anniversary Team. Rose's story reads almost identically on paper. History just chose to remember one of them differently.

There's no ACL to tear here. And for four games now, the player on the floor has looked exactly like the one the injury interrupted.

The Tuxedos, winners of four straight, currently sit second in the league standings. Second place doesn't rewrite any history books. But for a player the real game moved on from too early, it's hard not to notice a familiar shape returning — quietly, four games at a time.
