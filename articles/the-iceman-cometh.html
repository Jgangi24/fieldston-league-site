The Iceman Cometh

George Gervin is putting together an MVP case built on twelve unforgettable games for a Queens team that hasn't even been winning — and that's exactly what makes it real.

By Larry Kuppleton

Fieldston Core Four Fantasy League · August 7, 2026

There's a version of an MVP case that's easy to make and a version that's actually true, and George Gervin's is the second kind.

Over his last twelve games, Gervin is averaging 23.4 points and 9.3 rebounds a night for a Queens team that currently sits atop the league — numbers that would headline the conversation on their own.

But the case for Gervin isn't really about the average. It's about what happens when you actually watch the stretch unfold game by game: a 31-point explosion to steal a shootout from Blankendorf. A 22-rebound outburst from a guard-forward that has no business happening. A 12-of-14 night from the free-throw line in the moment his team needed him most.

“Ice doesn't get rattled,” said Queens team captain Bill Russell. “Whatever's happening in the game, his face doesn't change. He's just cold the whole time. That's why they call him what they call him.”

The record during this stretch is actually 5-7 — a fact that undercuts the easy narrative more than it helps it, and that's exactly why the case is worth taking seriously. Gervin hasn't been carried by a hot team riding a winning streak. He's been the singular bright spot on nights his team couldn't find another answer.

Game 33 is the signature performance of the run. Down the stretch of a 143-139 track meet against Blankendorf, Gervin went 8 of 18 from the field, hit 12 of 14 free throws, and finished with 31 points, 7 rebounds, and 2 steals. Queens needed every bit of it. He delivered every bit of it.

Off the court, teammates describe someone almost unrecognizable from the ice-cold competitor on it. “Funniest guy in the locker room, easy,” one teammate said. “You can't have a bad day around him — he won't let you.”

That sense of humor extends to hobbies most people wouldn't guess. Gervin has become something of a fixture at San Antonio's River Walk on off days, and has developed a genuine passion for painting small robot miniatures in his downtime.

Steve Nash, for one, can't quite explain it but respects it completely. “I don't fully understand the robots, if I'm being honest,” Nash said. “But I understand focus, and that's what that is for him.”

Tracy McGrady has a more personal relationship with the collection — or tried to, anyway. “There's this one robot, little red head on him, and Ice named him Bobert,” McGrady said, shaking his head at the memory. “I don't even know why, I just loved the name. Bobert. So I asked him, I said, ‘Ice, let me get Bobert.’ And he just looked at me. Then he goes, ‘No, T-Mac. Bobert stays with me.’ And I was like, alright. Fine, Ice.” McGrady paused. “I really wanted Bobert, though.”

The strangest story of the year, though, might be the one everyone in the locker room already knows by heart: Gervin reportedly auditioned for the role of the mutant Iceman in an upcoming X-Men film, on the strength of the nickname alone, only to be turned away for being too tall for the suit. He was then mistakenly sent a Comic-Con invitation anyway — one he says he's still deciding whether to use.

None of that changes what he's doing on the floor. Twelve games, 23.4 a night, a 22-rebound outlier that still doesn't quite make sense, and a signature performance in the moment his team needed it most. The Iceman cometh, indeed.
