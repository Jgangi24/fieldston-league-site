The Midseason All-Stars: Twenty-Four Deep, No Ground Given

Half a season is enough time to know who belongs on the biggest stage — the League's voting panel picked the 24 who earned it.

By Larry Kuppleton

Fieldston Core Four Fantasy League · August 3, 2026

FLAMINGTON — In a league that keeps score by the tenth of a rebound, half a season is plenty of time for the noise to settle. Twenty games in, every front office had a story it wanted to tell about its guy. Forty-eight games in, the numbers had told their own story instead — and the League's six-person voting panel spent the better part of a weekend arguing about the last two chairs before finally standing up from the table.

“Everybody thinks their guy is the snub,” said panelist Lukas Kowalczyk. “Then you put the GmSc next to the win column and about eighty percent of the arguments just evaporate.”

What was left standing: 24 players, split into two captained squads, drawn from all eight franchises in the standings — from Queens' league-best 29-19 to San Francisco's league-worst 9-40, which still managed to plant a name on the ballot. Below, the full rosters, with a word on how each player got here.

The Panel: Lukas Kowalczyk, Léon Kastellec, Lior Katz, Liam Kwan, Lars Kjeldsen, Luca Kovačević — ballots weighted, per league custom, toward GmSc with a team-success bonus. Ties broken by argument.

Team Gervin — captained by the League's leading MVP candidate, out of Queens.

Starters

George Gervin — GF, Queens (29-19) · 24.2 PPG, 19.6 GmSc

The clear No. 1 on every ballot the panel turned in. Gervin is averaging a league-best scoring mark for the league's best record, which is the entire argument for MVP in five words. Queens hasn't needed him to do it alone, but on the nights they have, he's obliged.

Magic Johnson — GF, Los Angeles (26-21) · 19.6/6.8/8.7, 18.3 GmSc

The only player in the top five who fills up a box score without ever needing to shoot it 20 times. Magic's assist number alone would start most nights; his rebounding total makes him the rare wing who can captain a defense from the perimeter.

Elgin Baylor — SF, Laurel Canyon (27-21) · 25.0 PPG, 17.1 GmSc

Nobody on either roster scores more efficiently at that volume. Baylor's been the engine of a Laurel Canyon team that's third in the conference despite a middling road record — which tells you where most of that damage gets done.

Russell Westbrook — PG, Blankendorf (23-25) · 17.3 GmSc season-long, 17 pts in Week 9 win over Oakland

Westbrook doesn't lead Blankendorf in scoring most nights, and that's precisely the point of a five-deep roster. He got his 17 in the win over Oakland without needing a single shot to be a hero shot — that's been the whole identity of a team that's clawed to sixth with no single star.

Bill Russell — C, Queens (29-19) · 15.5 REB, 3.9 BLK, DPOY frontrunner

The rebounding and shot-blocking numbers alone made him the runaway top seed on the Defensive Player of the Year ballot. Every possession Queens' offense doesn't have to defend after a Russell rebound is a possession the rest of the roster gets to spend on offense instead.

Reserves

Dwyane Wade — G, Queens (29-19) · 22.0 PPG, 15.9 GmSc

The clearest second scoring option on the league's best team, and a big reason Queens' top seed hasn't come with an asterisk about being top-heavy.

Kevin Garnett — FC, Flamington (28-20) · 18.2/11.8, 15.6 GmSc

A double-double most nights out of a frontcourt spot that's carrying real defensive weight — Garnett's rebounding and block numbers both rank inside the league's top ten.

Dominique Wilkins — SF, Jackson Heights (28-20) · 19.9/6.2, 15.3 GmSc

“The Human Highlight Film” nickname predates this league by decades, and Wilkins has spent this half-season reminding everyone why it stuck — 46.8% shooting at nearly 20 a night isn't an accident.

Scottie Pippen — SF, Oakland (22-26) · 18.6/5.6/2.0, 15.0 GmSc, Sixth Man frontrunner

Pippen's the rare bench piece who tops the Sixth Man ballot by a full two points and still shows up in winning-time box scores — 18 points, seven boards and five assists in the Week 9 loss to Blankendorf, in a game his own team didn't win.

James Worthy — F, Los Angeles (26-21) · 21.5/6.5, 14.8 GmSc

Third scoring option by name, first by efficiency — 46.2% from the floor on the third-most shots on his own roster.

Bob Cousy — PG, Blankendorf (23-25) · 15.3/8.3, 14.6 GmSc

“Nobody needs to have the best game of their life,” Cousy said after Blankendorf's win over Oakland this month. “We just need five guys to have a good one.” His own line that night — 18 points, 13 assists — was the proof.

Shaquille O'Neal — C, Laurel Canyon (27-21) · 12.8/13.3, 3.9 BLK, 14.1 GmSc

A 61.4% shooter anchoring the league's third-ranked defense from the five spot, and the rare center who still commands a double team on a roster full of them.

Team Rose — captained by the No. 2 MVP finisher, out of Flamington.

Starters

Derrick Rose — PG, Flamington (28-20) · 20.9/1.9/7.0, 18.4 GmSc

Second on the MVP ballot behind only Gervin, and by the panel's own admission, the closest anyone came to unseating him. Twenty points and seven assists a night from the point for a team sitting one game back of first.

Kawhi Leonard — SF, Oakland (22-26) · 24.9/3.9/2.9, 18.1 GmSc

The single highest-scoring efficiency profile outside the league's top four — even in a rough shooting night against Blankendorf (22 points on 6-of-15, five turnovers), the talent was never the question. The volume just hasn't always been kind lately.

Kobe Bryant — GF, Los Angeles (26-21) · 23.6/2.0/6.0, 17.7 GmSc

Third on the MVP ballot, and the only player in the top four averaging six assists a night on top of the scoring — a two-way backcourt threat that's kept LA in the playoff hunt despite a rough road record.

LeBron James — GF, Los Angeles (26-21) · 24.4/6.2/3.0, 16.9 GmSc

The most complete stat line of anyone on either roster — nobody else in the top ten touches his combination of scoring, rebounding and shot-blocking at the wing.

Michael Jordan — GF, Jackson Heights (28-20) · 27.3 PPG, 16.3 GmSc

The single highest scoring average in the league, full stop. Jordan's carrying a heavier shot volume than anyone else on this list, and the efficiency has mostly held anyway.

Reserves

Tracy McGrady — GF, Queens (29-19) · 23.6/2.1/1.8, 14.8 GmSc

The league's best record has two 20-point scorers in the starting five, and McGrady's the other one — a bucket-getter on a team that mostly wins by committee everywhere else.

Julius Erving — GF, Jackson Heights (28-20) · 19.9/5.2, 15.0 GmSc

“Dr. J” is still getting buckets at 48.8% from the floor, second-most efficient starter on a Jackson Heights team that's won eight straight.

David Thompson — GF, Laurel Canyon (27-21) · 20.4/3.9/2.1, 14.8 GmSc

The most complete guard on a Laurel Canyon roster loaded with frontcourt size — Thompson's the reason that team isn't just Baylor-and-O'Neal-and-hope.

James Harden — G, Flamington (28-20) · 20.6/3.8/5.3, 14.4 GmSc

Third scoring option, first in shot creation — Harden's assist number keeps a Flamington offense humming that already runs through Rose.

Jerry West — G, Laurel Canyon (27-21) · 14.8 PPG, 11.5 APG, 14.4 GmSc

The league's actual leader in assists per game, and it isn't especially close. West is the connective tissue in a Laurel Canyon lineup that scores from everywhere.

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander — G, Flamington (28-20) · 19.2/1.8/7.5, 13.8 GmSc

The last spot on either ballot came down to a coin-flip between SGA and one more name off a losing roster — the panel went with the guy helping a 28-20 team instead. Efficient scoring, elite assist rate, on a squad that's actually in the hunt.

John Havlicek — GF, Blankendorf (23-25) · 18.8/4.1/1.9, 13.9 GmSc

Nineteen points on 7-of-12 shooting, including three triples, in the Week 9 win over Oakland — the same “everybody chips in” formula that's kept a middle-of-the-pack Blankendorf team relevant in a crowded conference.

Twenty-four names, eight franchises, a nine-point spread in MVP scoring from top to bottom of the ballot — and still six people spent a weekend arguing about who got left off. That's the whole league in one sentence. Nobody needs to have the best half-season of their life. Half of these guys just needed one good enough night to make the panel remember.

Voting panel: Lukas Kowalczyk, Léon Kastellec, Lior Katz, Liam Kwan, Lars Kjeldsen, Luca Kovačević
