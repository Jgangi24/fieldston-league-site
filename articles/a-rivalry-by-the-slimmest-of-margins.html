A rivalry by the slimmest of margins

Two games, six combined points of margin, and a season series Laurel Canyon has swept despite their best player shooting 29% across both nights

By Larry Kuppleton

Fieldston Core Four Fantasy League · August 6, 2026

Six points. That's the entire margin separating Laurel Canyon and Flamington across two games and roughly four hours of basketball last week — a two-point escape, followed four days later by a four-point escape, and by the time it was over, there was no question left about whether these two teams have a rivalry. There was only the question of who's winning it.

So far, the answer is Laurel Canyon, and it isn't close in the standings even if it's been agonizingly close on the floor. The Chauffeurs have swept the season series, and with the two teams sharing the same division, every one of these meetings carries extra weight — Laurel Canyon now sits second in the league at 18-9, a full two-and-a-half games clear of a Flamington team that's lost three of its last four.

The stranger story is how Laurel Canyon actually won these two games, because their best player spent both nights in a shooting slump that would sink most teams outright. Elgin Baylor went 10-of-26 in the opener and followed it with a 5-of-25 nightmare in the rematch — 15 makes on 51 attempts across the two games, barely 29 percent. In most buildings, that's a loss. In this one, it was 2-0.

The reason is simple: everyone else showed up. Kevin Durant was close to flawless in the first meeting — 7 of 9 from the field, a perfect 3 of 3 from three, and a perfect 12 of 12 from the line, the kind of clean, surgical performance that makes a bad shooting night from your best player irrelevant. He followed it with another efficient outing in the rematch. Karl Malone bullied his way to a 28-point, 15-rebound double-double in Game 2. Anthony Davis was nearly automatic himself, 6 of 7 in the second meeting.

And then there's Jerry West, who might be the actual story of this rivalry more than anyone. Seventeen assists in the opener. Seventeen assists again four days later. Back-to-back 17-assist performances against the same opponent isn't luck — that's a point guard who has simply decided he's not letting this series slip, regardless of what his own star forward is shooting.

Flamington didn't lack for their own brilliance. James Harden went for 34 in the opener, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander answered with a stunningly efficient 29 on 7-of-9 shooting and a perfect 14 of 15 from the line. Marques Johnson exploded for 28 in the rematch, Derrick Rose added 27 on 9-of-13 shooting. Dwight Howard nearly had a triple-double's worth of impact without the scoring, grabbing 13 boards and blocking seven shots in the second game alone.

None of it was enough. Twice.

Laurel Canyon owner Mike Rubinstein wasn't shy about it afterward. “I don't need Elgin to go 26-for-26, I need him to go zero, and we still win by four,” Rubinstein said, sunglasses on indoors as always. “That's depth. That's a real organization. That's class. Something the Tuxedos know nothing about — look at their fans.” He didn't elaborate, and given what happened two sections over in the upper deck that night, he didn't need to. Mogul Mike, as always, found a way to stir the pot even after the final buzzer had already done most of the talking for him.

The players aren't immune to it either. “Me and Jerry used to get lunch in the offseason,” Derrick Rose said, shaking his head. “Not this year. I don't think I'm getting a Christmas card from that man, and I promise you he's not getting one from me.” Elgin Baylor, still stinging from the 5-of-25 night, was blunter. “Card list's getting real short around here. Hanukkah, Christmas, doesn't matter — Flamington's off it.” Jerry West, for his part, wouldn't confirm or deny the card situation, only smiling and saying, “We'll see who's still standing in April. That's the only card that matters.”

The heat wasn't contained to the box score, either. Arena security confirmed two separate incidents during the second meeting, including a shoving match in the upper-deck concourse between rival fan groups that required three staff members to break up. The more colorful of the two ended with a Flamington superfan — face painted half-red, rainbow clown wig somehow still intact — being led out in handcuffs, chin raised and defiant the entire way, refusing to so much as acknowledge the security guards on either arm.

League commissioner Jess Gangi didn't hold back when asked about it Monday. “When an owner behaves like that — and I think everybody's seen the photo by now — you're basically handing your fans a permission slip,” Gangi said. “You can't act like that courtside and then act surprised when things get out of hand in the stands. It starts at the top. I like Mike, I do, but he knows exactly what he's doing when he does stuff like that, and it's not doing this league any favors.”

Laurel Canyon owns this rivalry for now. Flamington has more than enough talent to flip it. With both teams jockeying for playoff seeding down the stretch, there's a real chance these two see each other again before the season's through — and if the last two games, and the two owners, are any indication, the temperature on this one isn't coming down anytime soon.
