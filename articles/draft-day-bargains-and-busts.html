Draft-day bargains and busts, checked against a real season

A season's worth of games is in the books — here's what the auction got right, what it got wrong, and which GM is quietly running the most efficient roster in the league

By Larry Kuppleton

Fieldston Core Four Fantasy League · August 5, 2026

The auction room is where every roster starts, and months later, the box scores get the final word. With a real season of games now on record, it's worth checking the actual results against what everyone paid on draft day.

The measuring stick here is simple: average Game Score — the same per-game production number already on every roster page — divided by draft cost. A high number means a team is getting a lot of production for not much money. A low number means the opposite.

The single best find of the entire auction wasn't close. Marques Johnson, a $1 flier for Jess's Flamington Tuxedos, is averaging an 11.2 Game Score across 21 games — production that would be a fair return at 10 or 15 times his actual price. Dolph Schayes ($1, San Francisco), Adrian Dantley ($1, Laurel Canyon), and Rick Barry ($1, Los Angeles) round out a group of true $1 fliers that all turned into real contributors.

The bust column tells a rougher story, and it's concentrated at the top of the market. Elvin Hayes went for $26 to Paul's Oakland roster and has produced a Game Score of 0.1 — functionally replacement level — across 20 games. Dave Cowens ($21, Jess's Blankendorf) and George Mikan ($27, Lew's Queens) are right there with him, each producing next to nothing on some of the bigger price tags of the night. Alonzo Mourning ($25, Jess) and Moses Malone ($36, Lew) round out a group of expensive bigs who simply haven't shown up.

Zoom out to the team level, and one roster stands apart. Jess's two teams are averaging 0.529 in Game Score per dollar spent across the board — comfortably the best mark in the league, and it's not just Marques Johnson padding the number. Lew sits second at 0.453, despite Steve Nash ($47, a 0.18 return so far) running well under what his price tag implied on paper. Mike is third at 0.426, and Paul brings up the rear at 0.414, weighed down by Stephen Curry ($49, 0.25) also running below expectations.

None of this is final — there's plenty of season left, and a cold stretch or a health scare can reshape any of these numbers in a hurry. But for anyone wondering whether the room got it right back on draft day, the early returns have some clear opinions.
